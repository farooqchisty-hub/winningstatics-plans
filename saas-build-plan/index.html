Winning Statics · SaaS build plan · 4 Oct 2026

Weekly Drops: the full build, from landing page to a product people keep paying for

Everything we need to build so the product works the way the staging landing page says it does: a weekly drop of ads built from what is winning in each merchant's niche, a niche report, videos, a monthly check of their own ads, a free plan, and the plumbing that keeps it reliable and profitable. Written from four seats: founder, product, engineering and design.

Staging landing page for review: ws-landing-staging.pages.dev · Personalised outreach view: ?s=gymshark.com · Earlier plans: Weekly Drops v1, design system

What we are building. A done-for-you ad subscription. Every Monday each merchant gets new static ads (and monthly videos) built from three inputs: their store, the ads their competitors keep paying to run, and our ranked library of winning templates. A free plan (5 ads a month) is the hook; four paid plans convert it.

Where we are. Ad generation, QA, dashboard, billing and email exist. The free plan is live end to end (claim, account, generation, delivery email, verified 4 Oct). Missing: the weekly engine (competitor intel, pattern matching, scheduled drops), renewals that actually deliver, the dashboard surfaces the page promises, the ad check, our own video renders.

The plan. Seven phases over about 9 weeks. Outreach can start after Phase 0 (about one week). Paying customers must have the weekly engine before their first renewal, which Phase 1 delivers by 24 Oct.

Unit economics. Estimated gross margin 62 to 76% on paid plans; a free store costs about $0.60 a month. Numbers to be confirmed by cost logging in Phase 0.

1. Founder view: the business we are building

Who it is for

Shopify and DTC stores already running Meta ads, 30 to 3,000 orders a month, founder or a one-person marketing team, no in-house designer. 6,591 named contacts are already ready for outreach.

The promise

"New ads every Monday, built from what is winning in your niche." Proof is provenance (days running, public Ad Library), never invented ROAS.

Why we win

Cheaper than Magritte ($99 to $349 a month) for more ads, done for you unlike AdBreezy, sourced and dated unlike every competitor, plus a free monthly check of the merchant's own results that no one else offers.

Moat over time

Every drop teaches us which patterns each merchant downloads, rebuilds and reports winning in their ad check. That data improves matching for the whole niche.

2. Product view: what the merchant experiences

Core loop

  1. Start free. Store URL and email. We read the store, propose 3 competitors and build the first 5 ads. Email when ready.
  2. First drop. Dashboard opens on "This week": 5 ads with copy, a niche snapshot (3 competitor ads that are lasting), and next month's drop shown locked.
  3. Upgrade. One recommended plan (Growth), monthly default, cancel in one click. Upgrade starts weekly drops the following Monday.
  4. Every Monday. New drop in the dashboard and inbox, with the niche report: what competitors kept running, what is new, which patterns we used.
  5. Every month. Videos land; the merchant uploads an Ads Manager export; we return scale, pause and fatigue calls, and the next drops lean on their winners.

Engineering principles (non-negotiable)

  • Durable async jobs. Every expensive step (intel pull, plan, generate, QA, video, email) is a row with a state machine (queued, running, done, failed, retry_at, attempts) and a retry ceiling. No work lives only in memory. Proven necessary by the meta-sweep (stuck queues, 409 replays).
  • Versioned context and lineage. Each drop records the product-context version, the patterns, templates and competitor ads it came from. Every ad can answer "why does this exist".
  • Entitlement ledger. Quotas are a ledger (granted, reserved, consumed, refunded), not counters scattered across orders. Fixes the renewals bug class for good.
  • Fail closed. QA errors hold an ad; payment and claim paths are idempotent; kill switches for free claims, marketing, intel and video.
  • One deploy path. Merge main before any backend deploy (4 Oct lesson); staging Worker gets real secrets so full paths can be tested off production.
  • Cost visible. Every external call logs cost against account and drop.

The engines

A. Store understanding (extend enrichProduct)

Read the store: products, photos, prices, claims, reviews, colours, fonts, logo. Output a versioned product context, including a proof inventory that decides which patterns are allowed (no review wall without reviews, no before and after without a visible change). Merchant confirms in onboarding.

B. Competitor and niche intel (ws-intel)

Discovery: Luna proposes competitors from the store's category and copy, resolved to Meta pages with the meta-sweep identity rules (landing-domain proof first, platform-link blocklist). Weekly pull per competitor plus one niche keyword search; media cached to R2; near-duplicate clustering (perceptual hash) counts variants; classification by Luna (copy: hook, angle, offer) and a vision model (format, visual style) into our 15-style taxonomy. Pattern score = days running × variants × still active × recency × niche relevance. Per-account cap about $0.05 a week. Shared cache: one niche pull serves every account in that niche.

C. Drop planner

For each account and week: quota from the ledger; mix about 50% competitor patterns that are lasting, 30% library winners for the category, 20% new angles; diversity rules (no more than 2 ads per pattern, rotate products, cover 1:1, 4:5, 9:16); ad check winners raise their patterns' weight. Output: one brief per ad (pattern, template id, product, angle, copy direction) queued for generation by Sunday night.

D. Generation and QA (existing, harden)

Template-led generation from the ranked library (never improvised prompts), product fidelity and OCR checks, claim check against product context, fail closed, auto-rebuild once, then hold for human review. First drop of every new account gets a human glance before release (sampling later).

E. Delivery and the niche report

Monday 10:00 UTC for every account (one global time, decided 4 Oct; it is Monday everywhere from Hawaii to New Zealand): drop published in the dashboard, email with 3 competitor ads that are lasting (dated, linked to the Ad Library), the patterns used, and the new ads. Late drop alert to Slack if any drop is not complete by 09:00 UTC, an hour before release.

F. Videos

Style templates from the MagicFit video library (22 styles), image-to-video from the merchant's product photo, scripted from the winning pattern, captions burned in, frame-sampled QA against the product photo, quota from the ledger, replaced by statics if QA fails. Replaces the 13 placeholder clips (production deploy is blocked until then).

G. Ad check

Upload an Ads Manager CSV (ad level, daily breakdown). Tolerant parser (column names, languages, currencies). Verdicts with minimum-spend thresholds: scale, keep, fatigue (CTR falling while frequency rises), pause (spend, no purchases). Report page and email; winners feed the planner. Later: partner access to their ad account once Meta verifies Millionth Mile Marketing.

H. Billing and entitlements

Dodo products for Starter, Growth, Scale, Studio (monthly and yearly). Webhook grants a period in the ledger; renewals grant the next period (this fixes the renewals bug); upgrades take effect next Monday with proration; downgrades and cancels at period end; failed payment pauses drops after a 3 day grace; cancel keeps all ads.

I. Lifecycle and growth

Free: first drop email, day 3 niche snapshot, day 7 "your next drop is locked" with the upgrade, monthly free drop email. Paid: Monday drop email, monthly video and ad check prompts, inactivity nudge after 2 unopened drops, cancel save (pause a month or drop a plan). Outreach attribution via the t token on every claim.

J. Admin and ops console

Queues and job health, late drops, QA hold queue, cost per account and per plan, intel coverage (accounts with fewer than 3 resolvable competitors), free-claim abuse view, manual overrides (re-run drop, grant ads, swap competitor). Slack alerts for failures, late drops and cost spikes.

Security, legal and compliance

  • Supabase magic-link auth, RLS on every table (verified), rate limits and Turnstile on public endpoints (live on the free claim since 4 Oct), CORS tightened to our origins, signed URLs for private assets.
  • GDPR: account deletion (currently mocked), data export, unsubscribe on every email, legal name Millionth Mile Marketing on terms and privacy (placeholders in terms.tsx still open).
  • Claims policy: provenance only ("running 143 days"), no invented performance, competitor creative shown only from the public Ad Library with source and date.
  • Placeholder videos: PLACEHOLDER_VIDEOS.txt blocks production landing deploys until our renders replace them.

6. Risks

What could go wrong, and the guard

  • Paid customers before the weekly engine. Guard: sell paid plans only after Phase 0; Phase 1 lands before any first renewal week.
  • Ad Library coverage gaps (competitors without a resolvable page). Guard: niche keyword pulls and library patterns fill the mix; never show an empty report.
  • Generation cost drift. Guard: cost ledger, per-account caps, alerts.
  • Quality complaints. Guard: fail-closed QA, human check on first drops, rebuild free, feedback loop.
  • Deploy regressions (4 Oct). Guard: merge main first, diff check, staging with real secrets.
  • Free plan abuse. Guard: one per domain, rate limit, Turnstile (live 4 Oct), daily cap, kill switch.
  • Compliance. Guard: provenance-only claims, legal pages, unsubscribe, deletion, placeholder guard.

What changed

  • The shake: the fixed background ran nine animated, masked layers behind every scroll frame. It is now one static layer that looks the same. Scroll reveals fade only (no sliding), and content already on screen is not faded at all.
  • Less motion: hero stack rotates every 5s with short tweens (was springs every 3.2s), pauses offscreen, no blur behind moving cards; section entrance animations removed; accordions that resized the page replaced by fixed layouts.
  • Lighter load: sections that are not on the page and the legal pages load only when needed; unused React Query, tooltip and toast providers removed; 11 decorative fonts no longer block first paint; Geist loads once.
  • Media: 320px copies of every library ad for small slots, competitor ads as WebP (975 KB to 264 KB), style videos recompressed (3.7 MB to 2.9 MB) with WebP posters, videos created only near the screen and at most 4 playing at once (2 on phones), long cache headers for videos, competitor ads and thumbs.
  • Minified: JS and CSS minified with no source maps, framework split into its own cached chunks, HTML shell minified at deploy.
  • Design: one shared instrument-panel kit (mono eyebrows, panel bars with live state, crop marks, data rows, segmented controls, hairline grids, Geist Mono for numbers) applied to every new section; the video row now sits inside the page column.
