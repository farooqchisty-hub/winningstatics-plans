Design system, visual language and copy style

The rules the landing page is built and rewritten against. Evolves the current look (near-black ground, mint accent, Geist) rather than replacing it, fixes the accessibility and consistency gaps an audit found, and sets the copy rules for cold-email traffic.

Staging: ws-landing-staging.pages.dev (noindex) · Production untouched · Sources are linked inline.

Visual idea: a calm, dark instrument panel where the only bright things are the merchant's ads and the one action we want. Mint means "act" or "proof", never decoration.

Copy idea: a founder talking to a founder who already runs Meta ads. Mechanism and offer first, every claim sourced, nothing invented.

Page logic: the visitor is solution-aware but has never heard of us (Schwartz), so the top of the page repeats the email's promise and the offer, and the rest earns belief in this order: how it works, a sample, the product, the price, the objections.

1. Principles

  1. One job per screen. Each section answers one question a buyer has, in the order they have it. If a section answers two, split it or cut one.
  2. The ads are the colour. The interface stays neutral so example ads carry the visual energy. Mint is reserved for the primary action, proof numbers and the one highlighted phrase per heading.
  3. Show the source. Every "winning" claim names where it comes from (the public Meta Ad Library, days running). Our honesty is the differentiator; no competitor dates or sources its claims.
  4. Accessible by default. WCAG 2.2 AA everywhere: 4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text and controls, visible focus, 24px targets (1.4.3, 1.4.11, 2.5.8).
  5. Fast on a phone from an email. The cold click lands on mobile more often than not; the form must be visible at 375px without scrolling, and LCP stays under 2.5s (Core Web Vitals).

2. Tokens

Three tiers, after the W3C Design Tokens format and the Material 3 and Carbon models: primitives (raw values), semantic tokens (meaning), component tokens. Components only ever read semantic tokens. Today's names (electric-indigo, verdant, iris) are purple-era names holding mint values; they become aliases of accent.* for one release, then retire.

3. Visual language by section

Order follows the Copyhackers message hierarchy with StoryBrand's "plan" and "stakes" beats inside it: offer, plan, sample, product, price, objections. Problem, Provenance, Steps, Case studies and Bonuses are out of the staging flow.

Nav

Job: orient and offer the action. Visual: floating glass bar, wordmark left, 5 anchors (How it works, Weekly report, Examples, Pricing, FAQ), one primary "Get 10 free ads". Rule: must not hide a focused element (2.4.11).

1. Hero: the promise and the form

Job: repeat the cold email's promise word for word, make the offer, take the claim. Visual: centred column on the ambient ground; ad rails on xl screens as moving proof; the h1 is the only large element, with the second line in mint. Form directly under a one-line subhead. A niche marquee replaces fictional brand logos. Personalised: ?s=store.com rewrites the h1 to "Ads for store.com, new every Monday." and pre-fills the field. Mobile: h1, subhead, both fields, CTA and the friction line inside 812px. Motion: reveal on load, rails at ambient speed, paused on hover and reduced motion.

2. How Weekly Drops work

Job: the plan, in four steps the visitor can repeat back. Visual: four equal cards, mint-tint icon circle, mono "Step N", short title, two-line body. A one-line "why longevity" argument under the h2. A small disclaimer line under the cards. Rule: each step names its source (the public Ad Library).

3. Sample weekly report

Job: process proof, the "labour illusion" (Buell and Norton): show the work. Replaces the fictional case studies. Visual: one panel styled like the real email: header with niche and date stamp, amber "Illustrative sample" label, left column data rows (days running bars, variants, source), pattern pills, right column example ads with EXAMPLE AD pills and a video tile. Rule: competitor names hidden, no performance numbers.

4. Before and after

Job: "picture your product in these". Keep as is: drag handle comparison, EXAMPLE AD treatment.

5. Styles and formats

Job: range. StyleExplorer tabs and the three-ratio diagram stay; copy shifts from "library" to "the formats your niche runs".

6. Dashboard

Job: what it is like to receive the ads. Keep the recorded library; future: add the "This week" tab once it exists.

7. Comparison

Job: anchor against agency, freelancer and tools. Only cite costs with a source on file (FTC substantiation).

8. Pricing

Job: make monthly the obvious choice. Visual: Starter strip above the four cards, monthly pre-selected on every card (default effect, Johnson and Goldstein), one-time visible beside it as the plainer option (decoy, Ariely), Growth raised with the single glow. Cancel terms in plain view (ROSCA). Open issue: struck-through "compare at" prices ($135 for $69) need a real former price on record, or they go.

9. The offer in six lines

Job: skim summary after the price. Six icon blocks, monthly framing.

10. Guarantee

Job: risk reversal we can keep: rebuilt free, no limit. Only time promises the pipeline meets.

11. FAQ

Job: objections in the order they arrive: what "winning" means, how competitors are picked, copying, what you get, cancelling, quality, the free pack, paying once, ownership. Answer in the first sentence.

4. Copy style

Voice

  • A founder talking to a founder. Contractions always. Sentences under 15 words, often under 8. Short paragraphs.
  • Concrete nouns: ad, Monday, your store, Ad Library, 90 days. Numerals, not words ("10", not "ten"), because digits catch scanning eyes (NN/g).
  • Answer in the first line of every section and every FAQ.
  • Grade 5 to 7 reading level: Unbounce's benchmark of 41,000 pages found 11.1% median conversion at that level against 5.3% for college level (Unbounce).
  • Plain language: short words, active voice, "you" (plainlanguage.gov).

Headlines

  • Mechanism plus outcome, front-loaded for F-pattern scanning (NN/g): "New ads every Monday. Built from what's winning in your niche."
  • Two-part h2: plain statement, then the mint payoff. One highlighted phrase per section, maximum.
  • The hero repeats the cold email's promise in the same words (message match).

CTAs

  • First person and outcome: "Get my 10 free BFCM ads". Test "my" against "your"; the classic lift does not hold everywhere.
  • Friction remover directly under the button: "No card. Ads in your inbox within the hour. One free pack per store."

Banned words and patterns

unlock, elevate, supercharge, game-changer, seamless, cutting-edge, revolutionize, harness, empower, effortless, robust, "in today's fast-paced", "take it to the next level", "not just X, it's Y", rhythmic lists of three, em and en dashes.

5. Pre-ship checklist

  1. Every text colour at or above white 50% (5.3:1); every control border at or above 3:1.
  2. One primary CTA per screen; mint used only for action, proof or the highlighted phrase.
  3. Every sample ad wears EXAMPLE AD; every sample report wears "Illustrative sample".
  4. No fictional brand shown as a customer; no invented metric anywhere.
  5. Hero form visible at 375 x 812 without scrolling; no horizontal scroll.
  6. Images carry width and height; LCP image prioritised; below-fold images lazy.
  7. Reduced motion respected; marquees pausable.
  8. Copy passes the banned list and the claims ledger.
